# 1.5 - Compare and contrast transmission media and transceivers
### - Wireless Networking
- 802.11 Standards
  - See 1.5 image
- Cellular
  - 4G & LTE: "Long Term Evolution"
    - converged GSM and CDMA providers
    - download rates of 150-300 Mbit/s
  - 5G
    - performance enhancements over 4G
    - speeds range from 100-900 Mbit/s
- Satellite
  - non terrestrial communication
  - higher cost than 4G/5G, less speed
  - used primarily for remote locations where terrestrial networking is unavailable
### - Wired Networking
- 802.3 standards
- fiber communication
  - transmission by light, good over long distances
  - single mode: only one light propagates through fiber, expensive light source, good over long distances
  - multi mode: multiple lights propagate through fiber, less expensive light source, runs up to 2 KM distance
- copper cabling
  - twisted pair: transmit+, transmit-, receive+, receive-
- coaxial cables
  - 2 or more forms share a common axis
- twin axial cables
  - 2 inner conductor
  - low cost & latency compared to twisted pair cables
- plenum space
  - room for cables and network infrastructure
  - cables are plenum rated to ensure they can sit in plenum space along with HVAC stuff and pipes and so on
### - Transceivers
- SFP (Small Form Factor Pluggable)
  - commonly used to provide 1 Gbit/s fiber,
  - SFP+ enhanced speeds up to 16 Gbit/s (usually 10 Gbit/s)
- QSFP (Quad SFP)
  - 4 SFP channels = 4 Gbit/s
  - QSFP+ gives 4 enhanced channels, up to 40 Gbit/s
  - cost savings in fiber cabling and equipment
### - Connector Types
- fiber
  - SC: Subscriber Connector, Square Connector, Standard Connector
  - LC: Local Connector, Lucent Connector, Little Connector, smaller and more compact
  - ST: Straight Tip
  - MPO: Multiple Fiber Push On, 12 fibers in a single connector
- copper
  - RJ11: Registered Jack type 11, 6 position 2 Conductor(6P2C), telephone/DSL connections
  - RJ45: Registered Jack type 45, 8 position 8 conductor(8P8C), Ethernet connection
  - F-type: coaxials cable standard connection type, common for cable TV
  - BNC: Bayonet Neill-Concelman, common with twin axial connections and DS3 WAN links
